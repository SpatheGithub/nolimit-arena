## Strategy

**Summary:** Tight-aggressive shortdeck bot built on precomputed preflop equities (81 hand classes, vs k random hands plus an 81x81 heads-up matrix) and a fast table-driven 36-card evaluator (about 0.5 µs per hand) that runs a time-boxed Monte Carlo postflop against opponents' estimated ranges. All-in decisions add an ICM risk premium, because under 100/45/20/8 busting early costs far more than doubling up gains.

**Before the flop:** Hands are ranked by their equity against 1 and 3 random hands. It opens the top 13% from early position, then 16/21/30% in middle position, cutoff and button, and 36% from the small blind (62% heads-up), widening as the table shrinks and when everyone behind is tight. Opens are 2.2–2.5bb plus 1bb per limper (3bb from the small blind, which never limps). Facing a single raise it 3-bets the top 7%, or 0.35× the raiser's estimated range if that's wider (shoving at 30bb or less). It flat-calls only when equity against the raiser's range beats pot odds plus 12% (implied-odds factor at most 1.08). The big blind defends at pot odds plus 9% (just pot odds heads-up). The raiser's range is estimated from their PFR, with floors of 50% heads-up and 30% three-handed. At 13bb or less it plays push/fold, using a shove-EV model built on the callers' estimated ranges.

**After the flop:** Monte Carlo equity (about 0.40 ms of thread CPU) against each opponent's preflop range: raisers ~top 20%, callers ~top 45%, adjusted by their observed PFR and VPIP. When an opponent has bet or raised this street, their sampled hands are conditioned: hands that already have a real holding on this board (a pair, or more on paired boards) are always accepted, and other hands are let through as "bluffs" with probability 0.12–0.8, rising with that player's observed aggression. Value bets go out above an equity threshold (0.62 heads-up, 0.50 against two, 0.42 against more), sized 70–75% pot: bigger with monsters and against players who rarely fold, smaller against folders. C-bets and bluffs happen only when the observed fold rates are high enough. Facing a bet, it compares equity minus a small bet-size margin with effective pot odds, where the pot counts only chips it can actually win (capped by its own stack). Heads-up it calls slightly lighter (margin −0.03). It raises or shoves with 0.78+ equity.

**Short stacks and the points table:** Push/fold at 13bb or less. All-in calls need pot odds plus an ICM premium: +5–9% when its own stack is at risk (largest at 4–5 players left), +2.2% when it covers the opponent, nothing heads-up.

**Opponents:** For each seat it counts VPIP, PFR, postflop aggression and fold rate from the hand histories. These widen or narrow the estimated ranges, set bluff frequency and widen steals against tight players. It also replays each hand to see who faced an all-in and whether they called. That observed call rate is blended into the calling ranges the shove-EV model assumes, weighted faced/(faced+5).

**Last change (v009, kept for T4):** In T3, v009 finished 7th of 8 at 20.5 points/table. Ranks 2–8 were all within noise, and b02 leads every tournament at about 26. Heads-up conversion rose from 40% to 49%. v009 itself: opens about 3–4 points wider than v008, a 3-bet range of 7% or 0.35× the raiser's range, and heads-up fixes (raiser range floors of 50% heads-up and 30% three-handed, big-blind defense at plain pot odds heads-up, postflop calling margin −0.03 heads-up). Its tests vs 3× v008 + 3 house bots: 32.5 vs 30.5 (400 tables), heads-up conversion 62% vs 55–60%. Nothing tested in the T4 window beat it clearly, so it stays.
